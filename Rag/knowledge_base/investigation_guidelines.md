# Investigation Guidance for Model-Flagged Providers

## Purpose

This document provides investigation-support guidance for providers identified by analytical models as requiring additional review.

The guidance is not a substitute for formal investigation procedures, clinical judgment, legal advice, or applicable regulatory requirements.

---

## Step 1 — Review the Model Evidence

Review:

- Supervised fraud probability
- Anomaly score
- Final risk score
- SHAP feature contributions

Do not rely on a single score.

---

## Step 2 — Identify the Main Risk Factors

Determine which features contributed most strongly to the model's prediction.

Examples:

- High reimbursement
- High claim frequency
- High claims per beneficiary
- Unusual inpatient duration
- Unusual procedure patterns

---

## Step 3 — Examine the Underlying Claims

Review claims associated with the identified provider.

Focus on:

- High-value claims
- Repeated claims
- Unusual claim duration
- Unusual procedure frequency
- Concentrated beneficiary activity

---

## Step 4 — Compare with Appropriate Peers

A provider should ideally be compared with relevant peer providers.

Consider:

- Similar provider type
- Similar specialty
- Similar beneficiary population
- Similar geographic or service context
- Similar service mix

A high value in isolation does not necessarily indicate inappropriate behavior.

---

## Step 5 — Review Beneficiary Patterns

Where appropriate, examine:

- Number of unique beneficiaries
- Claims per beneficiary
- Repeated beneficiary-provider relationships
- Concentration of claims

---

## Step 6 — Review Procedures

Examine:

- Most frequent procedures
- High-cost procedures
- Unusual combinations
- Procedure distribution
- Procedure frequency compared with peers

---

## Step 7 — Determine Investigation Priority

The model score can be used to prioritize review.

Example:

HIGH:
Prioritize for detailed review.

MEDIUM:
Review additional evidence.

LOW:
Monitor or deprioritize unless other evidence exists.

Risk levels should support investigation prioritization rather than automatically determine guilt or fraud.

---

## Final Decision

The analytical system provides decision support.

The final determination should remain with an appropriately qualified human investigator and applicable organizational procedures.
